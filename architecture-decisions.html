ARCHITECTURE DECISION RECORDS · PRODUCTION PROOF

The strongest architecture proof is a decision made under pressure.

Selected decisions from the published architecture record. Each one shows context, the choice, the alternative avoided and the production responsibility that followed.

Treat MedIQ as an operating ecosystem, not isolated modules.

National procurement, supply, inventory, assets, reporting and integrations had to evolve as one accountable transformation.

Context
Multiple workflows and institutional dependencies at national operating scale.
Decision
Govern architecture, migration, integration, testing, training, cutover and production as one path.
Alternative avoided
Feature-by-feature replacement that hides cross-domain dependencies.
Evidence
MedIQ case study →

Use contract-first integration across organisational boundaries.

UPA workflows needed to exchange procurement, warehouse and financial information with SAP while preserving clear responsibilities.

Context
Requests, receipts, invoices, statuses and reconciliation across separate environments.
Decision
API contracts, mapping, validation, security, SIT/UAT and reconciliation before production handover.
Alternative avoided
Direct field-to-field coupling with hidden data-quality ownership.
Evidence
Architecture page →

Keep domain ownership visible in Dynamics 365 integration.

Enterprise alignment was needed without making one platform imitate the other or duplicating business rules.

Context
Procurement, supply, inventory, finance and reporting workflows crossing platform boundaries.
Decision
Explicit interfaces, mapping, testing and handover while retaining visible business ownership.
Alternative avoided
Duplicated rules and conflicting sources of truth.
Evidence

Design production readiness alongside the solution.

Release, monitoring, support and continuity are not post-project administration; they are system requirements.

Context
Live services where failure has operational and institutional consequences.
Decision
Include readiness gates, observability, support ownership, escalation and stabilization in the delivery model.
Alternative avoided
Declaring success at deployment rather than at stable operation.
Evidence
