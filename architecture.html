MY ARCHITECTURE METHOD

One method. Five decisions.

Instead of separating “method”, “domains” and “decision model” into repeated explanations, I use one sequence that follows the life of a real solution from problem framing to accountable production.

Frame

Define the capability, stakeholders, current-state friction, constraints, dependencies and measurable outcome.

Bound

Set system responsibilities, source-of-truth ownership, shared capabilities and transition boundaries.

Contract

Define interfaces, mappings, validation, security, error handling and reconciliation before integration hardens.

Prove

Test functional and non-functional assumptions through SIT/UAT, performance, security and operational readiness.

Own

Make release, monitoring, support, escalation, continuity and post-go-live accountability part of the architecture.

ARCHITECTURE IN PRACTICE

Three cases. Real constraints. Explicit decisions.

The strongest architecture proof is not a framework diagram. It is the record of decisions made under delivery pressure, the alternatives deliberately avoided and the operating result that followed.

CASE 01 · MEDIQ

National healthcare procurement platform

MedIQ spans procurement, suppliers, delivery, inventory, assets, reporting and enterprise integrations. The architecture problem was therefore an operating-model transformation, not a standalone application release.

CONTEXTModernize at national operating scale.

Multiple workflows and institutional dependencies had to evolve while maintaining control, adoption and live-service continuity.

DECISIONTreat the platform as an integrated operating ecosystem.

Architecture, migration, integration, testing, training, cutover and production ownership were governed as one transformation path.

ALTERNATIVE AVOIDEDIsolated module replacement.

A feature-by-feature view would hide cross-domain data, integration and operational dependencies.

PRODUCTION OUTCOMEArchitecture carried through stabilization.

The platform moved through rebuild, reconciliation, readiness, cutover and live-service ownership with one accountable technology mandate.

EVIDENCEMedIQ case study →Project portfolio →External recognition →
CASE 02 · SAP STRATEGIC WAREHOUSES

Bi-directional institutional integration

The architecture challenge was to connect UPA workflows with a separate SAP warehouse environment while preserving clear contracts, master-data consistency, validation and production responsibility across organizational boundaries.

CONTEXTTwo institutions, one operational flow.

Requests, receipts, invoices, statuses and related procurement/financial data needed consistent exchange.

DECISIONContract-first integration with controlled environments.

API contracts, mapping, validation, security, SIT/UAT and reconciliation were treated as architecture concerns, with Dev → Pre-Prod → Prod progression.

ALTERNATIVE AVOIDEDDirect field-to-field coupling.

Tight coupling without explicit mapping and reconciliation would push data-quality and ownership failures into production.

PRODUCTION OUTCOMEGoverned handover across system boundaries.

Testing, deployment coordination and stabilization were built into the integration operating model rather than added after development.

EVIDENCEEnterprise integration projects →Flagship cases →
CASE 03 · MICROSOFT DYNAMICS 365

ERP integration without collapsing domain ownership

The objective was not to make one platform imitate the other. It was to integrate finance, procurement and inventory processes while preserving the responsibilities of the operational platform and the ERP.

CONTEXTOperational workflows needed ERP alignment.

Finance, procurement and inventory information had to move across MedIQ and Dynamics 365 with controlled ownership and testable interfaces. This is integration leadership; it does not imply ownership of the Dynamics 365 ERP/CRM implementation.

DECISIONKeep domain boundaries explicit.

APIs and integration mappings connect the systems while business ownership, validation and environment separation remain visible.

ALTERNATIVE AVOIDEDDuplicate business logic in both platforms.

Replicating rules across systems would create conflicting sources of truth and increase reconciliation cost.

PRODUCTION OUTCOMEA governed integration programme.

Blueprint alignment, interface definition, workshops, test environments and production-readiness activities were managed as one integration stream.

EVIDENCEDynamics 365 programme →MedIQ integration context →

PRODUCTION & GOVERNANCE

The architecture is incomplete until the service can be operated.

Production ownership is designed alongside the solution: readiness criteria, testing, monitoring, support, escalation, continuity and controlled change are part of the system, not post-project administration.

01

Quality gates

SIT/UAT, validation, reconciliation, performance and security checks before release.

02

Release readiness

Environment separation, cutover criteria, controlled deployment and stabilization.

03

Operational visibility

Monitoring, incident coordination, root-cause analysis, support ownership and escalation, with service and delivery signals including Flow/DORA metrics where applicable.

04

Continuity

Backup/recovery, resilience, RTO/RPO planning, fallback paths and accountable production ownership.
