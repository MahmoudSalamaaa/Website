MedIQ Core Modernization

Replacing a national operational platform without treating continuity, migration and production readiness as afterthoughts.

Enterprise architectureModernizationMigrationResilience

Context

MedIQ is a governmental procurement and supply-chain platform connecting procurement, supply, inventory, health entities, suppliers, integrations, governed workflows, data and executive visibility. The legacy environment required more than a cosmetic upgrade: the modernization was treated as a full re-architecture and replacement.

The constraint set

  • Operational continuity mattered throughout the transformation.
  • Migration required real data movement, validation and reconciliation rather than a clean-slate launch.
  • The target environment needed stronger scalability, maintainability, integration, reporting and operational control.
  • Delivery was performed 100% in-house with the technology organization.

Decision and transition strategy

The core rebuild took approximately three months, with about six months end-to-end including testing, training, migration, security/performance validation, readiness and cutover. Migration progressed gradually before a Big-Bang cutover, with approximately one month of parallel old/new operation.

Architecture and production-readiness model

Current evidence includes ReactJS/Node.js services, C#/.NET APIs, PostgreSQL, Redis caching, Docker/containerized services, F5 BIG-IP controls, monitoring/logging, production/replica database architecture, DR and off-site recovery. Redis is used as a caching layer, not represented as a job queue.

Outcome

The replacement reached end-to-end completion in approximately six months. Current evidence-safe MedIQ scale is 38,000+ users, 11,000+ healthcare facilities and 2,000+ suppliers. A separate FY2024/25 snapshot records 35,338 users, 5,991 requesting government entities, 2,155 suppliers, 89,761 warehouses/stores, 35,357 purchase orders and 938,242 recipient invoices. Those source periods are intentionally not merged.

What this case demonstrates

Enterprise modernization is not primarily a framework-selection exercise. The hard work is sequencing risk: migration, backward compatibility, data reconciliation, readiness, parallel operation, recovery and stakeholder adoption all influence the architecture.
