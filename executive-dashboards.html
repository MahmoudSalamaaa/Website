What Executive Dashboards Should Actually Do

An executive dashboard should compress complexity without hiding the decisions, exceptions and consequences that require leadership attention.

Start from decisions

Metrics are useful when they change an action, allocation or escalation. A dashboard designed around available data often produces visibility without decision value.

Show movement and context

A number without trend, target, denominator or operating context can mislead. Executives need to see what changed, why it matters and where intervention is required.

Make exceptions legible

Averages can conceal operational failure. Good dashboards expose material exceptions, bottlenecks, risk concentrations and unresolved dependencies.

Connect operational and financial views

Delivery, service, capacity and financial signals should not live in separate narratives when they describe the same technology system.

Preserve drill-down paths

Executive compression should not destroy traceability. Leaders should be able to move from a headline indicator to the evidence, owner and underlying operational detail.
