PRACTICE NOTE · PRODUCTION READINESS

When is an architecture actually finished?

Not when the diagram is approved. When the service can be released, operated, supported and improved with clear responsibility.

Architecture is often treated as a design milestone. In production environments, it is better understood as a responsibility system: what each part owns, how information moves, what can fail and who responds next.

Start with the failure you do not want to discover late.

A useful architecture conversation makes operational risk visible before release. That means asking where validation occurs, how reconciliation works, what happens when an external system is unavailable, and who has the authority to pause or recover a workflow.

Readiness is part of the design.

  • SIT and UAT prove functional assumptions.
  • Performance and security checks test non-functional assumptions.
  • Cutover criteria make the transition reversible and accountable.
  • Monitoring, escalation and support ownership define the service after launch.
A feature is not a service until someone can operate it under pressure.
