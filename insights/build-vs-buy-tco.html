Build vs Buy: Use TCO, Capability Fit and Dependency Risk

Technology selection is an investment decision, not a feature comparison.

Build-vs-buy decisions become weak when the conversation stops at license price or initial development effort.

Use lifecycle economics

Consider licensing, infrastructure, implementation, integration, support, maintenance, staffing, upgrades, training and exit cost. In government environments, procurement constraints and vendor dependency also influence the real lifecycle cost.

Capability fit matters more than checkbox parity

A product can have more features and still be the wrong choice if it introduces integration complexity, operational burden or a dependency the organization cannot support.

Use PoCs when uncertainty is material

A small prototype is useful when it tests a risky assumption: performance, interoperability, deployment fit, maintainability or team capability. A PoC should answer a decision question, not become a demo ritual.

Takeaway

The right technology is the one whose benefits survive contact with lifecycle cost, operating constraints and strategic dependency.
