Leadership & decision model

Technical authority without architecture theatre.

I treat architecture as accountable decision-making: clarify the problem, expose trade-offs, choose deliberately, govern execution, validate production readiness and learn from operational evidence.

Decision model

A practical operating rhythm.

Formal governance is useful when it adds traceability; ceremony that does not improve a decision is not the goal.

Business outcome, constraints, users, regulation and operating context.
Alternatives, architecture risk, dependencies, technical debt and TCO.
PoC/prototype when uncertainty justifies it.
Decision with the relevant technical team and clear ownership.
Implementation, QA/UAT, performance/security validation, cutover and feedback.

Technology strategy

Translate organizational priorities into target-state direction, roadmaps, investment sequencing and capability evolution across applications, data, integrations, infrastructure and security.

Architecture ownership

Own solution and enterprise architecture decisions while distinguishing personal hands-on implementation from team implementation under technical leadership.

Build vs buy / TCO

Evaluate capability fit, lifecycle cost, implementation/support burden, vendor dependency and strategic fit rather than selecting by feature lists alone.

Risk & resilience

Consider scalability, single points of failure, security, migration, vendor dependency, technology lock-in, recovery and production readiness as first-class architecture concerns.

People & operating model

Set technical ownership, grow capabilities and align engineering, QA, DevOps, data, infrastructure, security and support around shared outcomes.

Executive alignment

Present options, trade-offs, priorities, risks and investment choices in business language while preserving the technical integrity of the decision.

Principle

Accountability matters more than vocabulary.

I do not claim production FHIR where it was not implemented, microservices where the evidence does not support it, or personal coding ownership where a team implemented the solution.
