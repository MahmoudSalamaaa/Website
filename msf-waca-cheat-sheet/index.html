3. What do you know about the history of MSF?
1971 • Paris • Biafra • Doctors & journalists • Nobel 1999
MSF was founded in Paris in 1971 by doctors and journalists after the war and famine in Biafra, Nigeria. They wanted an independent organization that could provide emergency medical help quickly and without discrimination. MSF later grew into a global medical humanitarian movement and received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1999.
4. What do you know about MSF and its work?
Medical humanitarian work • Emergency care • Independent • Need • Speaking out
MSF is an international medical humanitarian organization. It provides medical care to people affected by conflict, disasters, disease outbreaks and other crises. It works based on people's needs and aims to stay independent from political, economic and religious interests. MSF can also speak out when its teams witness serious suffering or barriers to medical care.
5. What are the main MSF principles?
Impartiality • Independence • Neutrality • Medical ethics • Speaking out
The three core MSF principles are impartiality, independence and neutrality, together with strong medical ethics. Impartiality means helping people based on need, without discrimination. Independence means decisions are based on medical and humanitarian needs, not political, economic or religious interests. Neutrality means MSF does not take sides in a conflict. Medical ethics means putting patients first, protecting confidentiality and doing no harm. MSF may also speak out when its teams witness serious suffering or barriers to medical care.
6. What do you know about MSF WaCA?
West & Central Africa • Operational association • Proximity • Innovation • Digital transformation
MSF WaCA means West and Central Africa. It is an operational association within the MSF movement, created to strengthen leadership and decision-making from West and Central Africa. Its approach focuses on being close to patients and communities, inclusion, innovation and digital transformation. For me, this is important because the Information Systems Manager role directly supports reliable and secure operations in that mission.

26. Explain the 3-2-1 rule, immutable backup and a failed backup scenario.
3-2-1 • Immutable • Offsite • Restore test • Escalation
The 3-2-1 rule means keeping three copies of the data, on two different types of storage, with one copy offsite or isolated. An immutable or offline backup helps protect against ransomware. If a backup fails, I escalate it, understand the risk, fix the backup process, confirm the next backup works and test that we can restore the data.

Finally, I fix the root cause and improve the controls to reduce the chance of it happening again.
