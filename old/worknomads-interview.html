Do not say: “I have 18 years of Enterprise Architecture experience.” Say you have 18+ years in technology, progressing into enterprise architecture and leadership.

4. Core interview answers

Why WorkNomads?

What attracted me is not simply that the position is remote. The role sits at the intersection of enterprise architecture, digital transformation, data, cloud and emerging technologies, which is the direction I want to continue developing in. I also value the international environment and the opportunity to apply my experience across more diverse enterprise contexts.

Why this role now?

My career has evolved from hands-on software engineering into architecture and technology leadership. I’m now looking for a role where architecture is the central responsibility — connecting business strategy, enterprise systems, integration, data, governance and transformation at a broader international scale.

Largest transformation?

I tend to approach transformation as an ecosystem rather than as individual applications. I identify business capabilities, systems of record, integration boundaries, data ownership, security requirements and operational constraints, then define the target architecture and move toward it incrementally rather than through a risky big-bang transformation.

Architecture philosophy

Architecture must be actionable. I balance business outcomes with security, reliability, interoperability, cost, maintainability and delivery reality. A good architecture decision should help teams build and operate systems better — not just produce diagrams.

6. Manufacturing architecture in 90 seconds

Think in layers

Shop floor: PLCs, sensors, machines → Control: SCADA/DCS → Operations: MES & historians → Enterprise: ERP, supply chain, CRM → Data/Cloud: lakehouse, analytics, AI.

Architect’s job: define boundaries, secure IT/OT integration, decide what runs at edge vs cloud, standardize data and interfaces, preserve plant availability, and govern change without disrupting production.
