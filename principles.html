OPERATING PRINCIPLES

How I think about technology decisions.

Principles are useful when the roadmap changes. These are the lenses I use to connect engineering, delivery and institutional outcomes.

01

Start with the operating problem, not the technology.

A platform is valuable only when it changes a real workflow, decision, service or outcome.

02

Architecture is a set of decisions.

Diagrams matter, but trade-offs matter more: boundaries, ownership, integration, failure modes, data, security and change.

03

Scale changes the definition of “working.”

At institutional scale, adoption, support, auditability, resilience and governance are part of the product.

04

Evidence beats adjectives.

Prefer measurable scope, outcomes, operating data and traceable artifacts over claims such as “innovative” or “world-class.”

05

Build capability, not dependency.

Vendor relationships should transfer knowledge, clarify accountability and leave the organization stronger.

06

Governance should accelerate good decisions.

Controls work best when they make ownership, risk, cost and decision rights visible without turning delivery into bureaucracy.

07

AI needs grounding before glamour.

Enterprise AI should be designed around trusted sources, evaluation, access controls, traceability and human judgment.

08

Keep the engineering feedback loop alive.

Executive leadership should not lose contact with system behavior, architecture constraints and delivery reality.

These principles describe a working method—not a maturity score or a list of buzzwords.
